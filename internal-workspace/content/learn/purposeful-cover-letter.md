# Give a cover letter one clear purpose

A cover letter connects your real experience with the employer’s stated needs. It should add a reason to consider your application, rather than reproduce every CV bullet or invent a hidden company problem.

## Use a simple structure

Name the role and company. Choose one or two relevant examples from your background. Explain how those examples relate to the advertised responsibilities, then close with a clear interest in discussing the role.

If you research the company, separate published facts from your interpretation. You can say a responsibility interests you without claiming to know a confidential weakness. Avoid promises about results you cannot guarantee.

Review the company name, role, tool claims and dates before using a generated draft. Keep the length appropriate to the employer’s instructions. When an employer asks for your own unaided writing, complete that requirement yourself. An attached reviewed letter is a separate snapshot, so later edits require another review.
